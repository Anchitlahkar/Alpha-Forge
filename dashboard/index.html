Rollback: Experience Backtracking in World Models for Policy Improvement

9.15Ranked

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers · signal 9.0/10

A new method called Rollback improves robot policies by letting world models rewind to past decisions, explore different actions, and learn from counterfactual outcomes, leading to better performance and faster training.

So what

Robot policies can now learn more effectively by exploring alternative past actions in simulated environments. This allows for faster and more stable improvement, even surpassing human performance in some tasks.

Key points

  • Rollback is a new method that improves robot policies by using world models to rewind to past decisions.
  • It explores counterfactual actions and generates fine-grained supervision without needing a separate value model.
  • The system unifies distributed world-model search with asynchronous real-robot execution, forming a self-reinforcing cycle.
  • Real-world experience continually refines the world model, and a sharper world model drives further policy improvement.
  • Rollback improved success rates over baselines by 10.67% on block extraction, 11.11% on ball tossing, and 11.85% on tabletop curling.

  • Look up Jiayi Luo, one of the authors, to see their other research.

The Trinity: Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers

The Trinity framework enables multimodal AI agents to recursively improve themselves by organizing tasks into capability trees, generating simulated training worlds, and evolving specialized harness modules, leading to significant performance gains on unseen tasks.

  • The Trinity framework allows multimodal agents to recursively improve their own capabilities.
  • It organizes tasks into a "capability tree," a hierarchy based on shared underlying requirements rather than surface categories.
  • The system uses "Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real" to abstract common task requirements into simplified, verifiable simulated worlds for learning.
