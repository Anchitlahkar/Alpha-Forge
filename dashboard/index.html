Netflix's Multimodal Asset Personalization (MAPS)

8.45Ranked

AI Research · Netflix Tech Blog · 28 Aug · signal 8.0/10

Netflix has implemented MAPS, a system utilizing multimodal embeddings (like CLIP) to enhance asset personalization by allowing models to process both visual and auditory content. This innovation addresses the cold-start problem for new titles, unifies models across various display canvases, and enables query-aware personalization, ultimately improving user experience and operational efficiency.

So what

Multimodal embeddings allow personalization systems to understand content directly, reducing reliance on historical interaction data. This enables immediate, taste-based recommendations for new content and improves model efficiency by consolidating multiple specialized models into one.

Key points

  • Netflix implemented multimodal embeddings to enhance asset personalization, moving beyond opaque asset IDs.
  • They use CLIP, a pretrained image-text embedding model, to encode artwork, concatenating its 768-dimensional image embedding with a learned ID embedding.
  • This approach addresses the cold-start problem for new assets, applying member taste signals immediately based on visual themes, talent, and color palettes.
  • CLIP embeddings enable model consolidation, replacing five per-canvas models with one unified model that pools interaction signal across all display canvases.
  • Reward-based weighting, tied to long-term member satisfaction, mixes training data from disparate canvases, preventing high-volume interactions from dominating.

Worth doing

  • Read the CLIP paper to understand its architecture and capabilities: `arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020`.
  • Investigate inverse propensity scoring (IPS) for unbiased offline evaluation in your own recommendation systems; start with `arxiv.org/abs/1602.05352`.
  • Look into Netflix's long-term reward modeling for weighting training data: `netflixtechblog.com/recommending-for-long-term-member-satisfaction-at-netflix-ac15cada49ef`.

UrbanGround MLLM Research

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers

Researchers developed UrbanGround, a 3D replica of Hong Kong, to evaluate multimodal language model (MLLM) agents. The study found that MLLMs excel at local perception but struggle with long-range navigation and adapting to dynamic urban changes.

  • UrbanGround is a sandbox using a georegistered 3D replica of Hong Kong for testing multimodal language model (MLLM) agents.
  • It allows closed-loop interaction from a first-person view and includes an interactive map.
  • Current MLLM agents show strong local perception and short-range spatial reasoning.
