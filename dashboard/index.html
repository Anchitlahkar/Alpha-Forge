Lieb-Schultz-Mattis Constraints for Quantum Channels

8.22Ranked

Quantum Computing · arXiv quant-ph · 28 Aug · signal 8.0/10

This paper introduces a spacetime-duality view to map open quantum channels to higher-dimensional topological phases. It presents new diagnostics, including the twisted Renyi-N correlator and the Liouvillian singular spectrum, to probe temporal correlations and quantum anomalies.

So what

Understanding how quantum anomalies constrain open quantum systems helps in designing and controlling quantum devices. This improves characterization of complex quantum states.

Key points

  • Quantum anomalies, such as those described by the Lieb-Schultz-Mattis (LSM) theorem, strongly constrain many-body quantum systems.
  • The research uses a spacetime duality to map the Liouvillian of a d-dimensional repeated quantum channel with a mixed anomaly to a (d+1)-dimensional mixed-state symmetry-protected topological (mSPT) phase.
  • The initial and steady states of the quantum channel are identified with boundary states of the higher-dimensional mSPT phase.
  • The twisted Renyi-N correlator (TRNC) is introduced as a new probe for temporal correlations in the channel.
  • TRNC is dual to the mSPT strange correlator. This provides a direct way to diagnose long-range temporal order implied by the LSM anomaly.

  • Investigate "mixed-state symmetry-protected topological (mSPT) phases" to grasp the higher-dimensional mapping concept.
  • Research the "twisted Renyi-N correlator (TRNC)" and the "Liouvillian singular spectrum" as new diagnostics for quantum systems.

Fast GHZ State Encoding in Quantum Computing

Quantum Computing · arXiv quant-ph

Researchers have proven that N-qubit Greenberger--Horne--Zeilinger (GHZ) state encoding can be achieved with high fidelity (1-o(1)) in O(log N/N) time, matching the theoretical minimum speed.

  • N-qubit Greenberger--Horne--Zeilinger (GHZ) state encoding can be performed with (1-o(1))-fidelity.
  • The encoding time is O(log N/N) using all-to-all 2-local Hamiltonians with bounded 2-qubit interaction terms.
  • This time complexity saturates the theoretical lower bound of Ω(log N/N).
