ZimaBlue: Evolving Generalizable World Action Models through Scalable Video Pre-training

8.8Ranked

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers · signal 8.5/10

ZimaBlue is an AI framework that uses large-scale egocentric video pre-training and a novel Slow-Fast architecture to substantially improve zero-shot robotic manipulation.

So what

Robots can learn complex manipulation tasks more effectively from vast amounts of unlabeled video data. This reduces the need for expensive, diverse action-labeled trajectories.

Key points

  • ZimaBlue learns generalizable World Action Models (WAMs) from large-scale egocentric video, addressing the challenge of converting action-free experience into robot control.
  • It employs a three-stage training curriculum: causal embodied video pre-training, video-action mid-training with a unified action representation, and specialization for a target robot.
  • The framework uses a Slow-Fast dual-system architecture; a high-capacity Slow world model provides generalizable spatiotemporal representations, and a lightweight Fast branch enables 30 Hz action prediction on an NVIDIA RTX 4090.
  • Scaling from target-robot data alone to over 120,000 hours of embodied video improved real-robot zero-shot evaluation success from 36.1% to 77.8%.
  • ZimaBlue delivers strong performance across multiple benchmarks, with notable gains on unseen tasks.
