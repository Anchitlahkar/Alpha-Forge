AgentGarten: Code Worlds for Evolving Agents

9.22Ranked

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers · signal 9.0/10

AgentGarten is a framework that combines simulators and neural rendering to create real-time interactive environments for AI agents, enabling them to learn complex tasks in just 4 rounds of experience, a substantial improvement over traditional methods.

So what

This framework allows AI agents to learn complex behaviors in interactive environments with significantly less training data. It could accelerate the development of more capable and adaptable AI systems.

Key points

  • AgentGarten is a framework for building real-time interactive environments where AI agents can evolve.
  • It couples traditional simulators and game engines with a shared neural renderer to generate realistic visual observations.
  • The neural renderer uses a technique called Adversarial Forcing, which makes history prefilling differentiable and adds real-data adversarial supervision.
  • Agents perceive the world visually, interact in real-time, and improve by distilling experience into playbooks that subsequent agents inherit.
  • Empirical studies show agents learn tasks in 4 rounds, a significant efficiency gain compared to millions of rounds for conventional reinforcement learning.

TokenRouter: Efficient Serving System for Token-Level LLM Routing

AI Research · Hugging Face Papers

Researchers developed TokenRouter, a new serving system for token-level LLM routing that achieves 2 to 64 times higher decoding throughput than current systems by using a request-centric, model-centric approach and delayed-batching schedulers.

  • Token-level routing for large language models (LLMs) offers efficiency and quality gains over coarser-grained methods.
  • Existing LLM serving systems struggle with token-level routing due to single-model assumptions, step desynchronization, and batch admission delays.
  • TokenRouter is a new system designed for efficient token-level LLM inference.

On linearity or non-linearity in machine learning for quantum chaotic dynamics

AI Research · arXiv quant-ph

A study found that a simple linear machine learning model, DLinear, accurately forecasts chaotic quantum many-body dynamics across various regimes, outperforming a complex Transformer model. This suggests simpler models can predict complex quantum behavior.

  • Simulating chaotic quantum many-body dynamics is a major computational challenge for classical methods.
  • Researchers explored machine learning for time-series forecasting of quantum dynamics.
  • They used a few-qubit PXP chain, realizable with Rydberg-atom arrays, as a benchmark system.
